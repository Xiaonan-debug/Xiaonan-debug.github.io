SecProbe: Adaptive Evaluation of Coding Agents on Cybersecurity Vulnerabilities

Xiaonan Luo, Yue Huang, Kehan Guo, Ping He, Chuan Zou, Chujie Gao, Lichi Li, Yuchen Ma, Zhangchen Xu, Zichen Chen, Yufei Han, Xiangliang Zhang

Static benchmarks spend most of their budget on tasks that tell us little about a given agent. SecProbe treats evaluation as measurement: it uses Item Response Theory to estimate a coding agent's ability on the fly, selects the repository-scale vulnerability-repair tasks that are most informative at that ability level, and synthesizes new executable tasks, with exploit-based tests and reference patches, when the existing pool lacks coverage.

IdeaTasks whose difficulty sits near an agent's current ability estimate carry the most information. SecProbe evaluates on those tasks and synthesizes new ones when the pool has gaps.

02arXiv · Jul 2026

Learning from Synthetic Data without Model Collapse in Iterative Instruction Tuning

Xiaonan Luo, Yue Huang, Kehan Guo, Ping He, Chuan Zou, Ting Hua, Xiangliang Zhang

Training a model on its own synthetic data, generation after generation, is widely expected to cause collapse. Using DINA-based skill diagnosis, we show that collapse in iterative instruction tuning is a polarization of competence: synthetic training reinforces skills that are already strong and erodes those that are weakly supported. We propose KITE, which keeps improvement stable across generations.

  • Weakness-guided generationDiagnoses which skills a model lacks and generates candidate instructions that target them.
  • Boundary-aware curationKeeps examples near the model's uncertainty boundary, where training signal is highest.
  • Stable over generationsMore stable multi-generation gains than strong synthetic-data baselines across reasoning benchmarks.
Paper ↗PDF ↗

FindingIllustration. Naive self-training pulls strong and weak skills apart over generations; KITE raises both.

03AAAI 2026

Better Datasets Start From RefineLab: Automatic Optimization for High-Quality Dataset Refinement

Xiaonan Luo*, Yue Huang*, Ping He, Xiangliang Zhang

Benchmarks drift away from what they are meant to measure. RefineLab treats QA dataset refinement as an optimization problem: given explicit quality targets and a token budget, it formulates refinement as an integer linear program that selects which coverage, difficulty, and factual-consistency edits to apply, so the refined dataset matches the targets.

  • Refinement as an ILPChooses edits under a token budget to meet explicit coverage, difficulty, and consistency targets.
  • Modular operatorsRefinement operators that can be combined and evaluated independently.
  • Validated on standard benchmarksStable evaluation improvements on MMLU, GSM8K, and HumanEval.

MethodSchematic. Candidate edits are scored against quality targets; an ILP picks the best set within the token budget.

Research

I work on how language-model systems can improve themselves and act reliably in high-stakes settings.

  1. 01

    Recursive self-improvement

    Systems that get better by improving themselves, from research agents that keep findings across sessions and verify progress against fixed evaluators, to models trained on their own data without collapsing.

  2. 02

    Cybersecurity

    Measuring and improving how coding agents find and repair real-world vulnerabilities, with executable tasks, exploit-based tests, and adaptive evaluation.

  3. 03

    Agents

    LLM agents that operate over long horizons, including agent harnesses that learn from experience and the risks that emerge when many agents interact.

  4. 04

    Post-training

    Post-training that makes models more capable and reliable, including adaptive reasoning strategies and rewards for answers that are right for the right reasons.

  5. 05

    Synthetic data

    When synthetic data helps and when it hurts, and how to generate, curate, and refine data around a model's actual weaknesses and an evaluation's actual goals.
